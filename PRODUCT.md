# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Busy professionals, students, and individual productivity users who need a fast, focused place to manage their daily work. They open this landing page while evaluating whether TaskFlow is worth their trust. They are tired of bloated, corporate-feeling tools. They want something that respects their intelligence and feels like it was built for people who get things done, not for enterprises buying software by committee.

## Product Purpose

TaskFlow is a personal task management SaaS. It helps individuals create, organize, prioritize, and complete tasks from any device, with zero friction. The landing page and public pages exist to earn that first registration click. The app itself is the product; the marketing surface must make the app feel inevitable.

Currently free for all users. Paid tiers are planned. The marketing pages must communicate quality and confidence without overselling features that don't exist yet.

## Brand Personality

Motivational, sharp, confident.

- **Motivational**: the interface should make you feel capable, not managed. Every headline earns the user's energy.
- **Sharp**: precise copy, clean decisions, no filler. Like a tool that was made with intention.
- **Confident**: no hedging, no "we think you might like", no softened language. Raycast energy.

Reference: Raycast. Dark, keyboard-forward, editorial typography, confident negative space, strong weight contrast, purposeful reveals. Not mimicry — shared stance.

## Anti-references

- **Microsoft To Do**: corporate, generic, committee-designed, soft typography, diluted color. The exact opposite.
- **Todoist**: bubbly, consumer-grade, tries to be friendly to the point of feeling childish.
- **Any SaaS landing page** with: identical-card feature grids, hero-metric templates (big number + small label + gradient accent), generic "boost your productivity" headlines, large rounded icons above headings.

## Design Principles

1. **The app is the hero.** Show the dashboard. Don't describe features in prose when you can show the interface working.
2. **Earn every word.** Copy must be specific, confident, and brief. No restated headings. No "we're excited to announce."
3. **Restraint is not timidity.** Negative space is active voice. A sparse layout that makes one thing land is better than a dense layout that says ten things at once.
4. **Legal pages stay on-brand.** Terms of Service and Privacy Policy should be clean and readable but live in the full brand shell. A user reading your ToS should still feel they are in the same product.
5. **Dark first, always.** The scene: someone evaluating a productivity tool at their desk, late afternoon, ambient light off. The dark theme is not a mode — it is the brand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AAA target across all public pages.
- Contrast ratios: 7:1 minimum for body text, 4.5:1 minimum for large text and UI elements.
- Full keyboard navigation on landing page interactive elements.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — disable entrance animations when set.
- Color is never the only information carrier.
- All images and icons carry descriptive alt text.
